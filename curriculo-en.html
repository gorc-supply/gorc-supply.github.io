SupplyGraph OPME · Neo4j
Graph digital twin of a multi-tier medical-device supply network
Live
  • Synthetic network calibrated with open data (Brazilian import statistics, NCM codes, IBGE) in Neo4j: tier-1/2/3 suppliers in 17 countries, 1,400 hospitals and 58k traceable lots. In Cypher it shows what the ERP misses: 66 of 224 dual-sourced components are effectively single-source and feed 53% of revenue; China dependence rises from 0.4% (tier-1 view) to 43.6% (full network).
  • Graph Data Science (PageRank, betweenness, Leiden, Node Similarity, FastRP) feeding a supplier criticality index, disruption simulation with Time-to-Survive × Time-to-Recover and lot recall in 19 ms; benchmarked against recursive SQL in DuckDB with identical answers in all 18 scenarios.
Supply OPME
Supply chain SaaS for medical-device distributors
Live
  • Web system with demand forecasting, purchasing, lot and ANVISA-registration traceability, hospital consignment and S&OP. Forecasts by moving average, SES, Croston-SBA and TSB, picked by backtest; I checked the engine against a second one written separately in Python: 474 identical forecasts.
  • AI assistant (Claude, via the Anthropic API) that answers questions on stock, lots and expiry dates by querying the database with the asking user's login (Postgres row-level security): read-only, shows what it queried, under US$ 0.01 per typical question and a spending cap per company.
  • Dashboard KPIs went from 31 s to 0.5 s after query optimization; 1,797 automated database and browser tests on every push.
Data Robot · Supply OPME
Data quality before anything is loaded
In use
  • Desktop app that checks the client's ERP exports against 86 rules in 12 families (structure, format, dates, duplicates, calculations and statistics: values out of the item's pattern and drift between loads). It caught 90 of 90 planted errors in 15 s; 234 automated tests.
  • It never edits the data: only records that pass are loaded, and the client gets a report to fix the errors at the source. Built with AI coding agents; any new rule the robot suggests stays off until I validate it.
A/B test of replenishment policies
Python · NumPy · SciPy · synthetic data
2026
  • Experiment on 600 SKUs stratified by ABC class: min-max rule against a forecast-based order-up-to policy (Croston-SBA). Primary metric, guardrail and minimum effect set in advance; sample size computed; A/A test with a 3.5% false-positive rate.
  • Read with Welch, bootstrap, permutation, Mann-Whitney, CUPED and Holm correction: fill rate +3.1 pp (95% CI +1.4 to +4.7) with 15% less capital, plus a per-class recommendation.
