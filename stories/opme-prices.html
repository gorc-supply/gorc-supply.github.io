What Brazil's public sector pays for implants

Hospitals buy stents, pacemakers, screws and plates through public tenders, and every purchase is published. I pulled two years of them, adjusted for inflation, and crossed them with import data and device registrations. The headline is less about overpricing than about what a catalog code hides.

Brazil buys its implants from abroad

OPME is the Brazilian acronym for orthoses, prostheses and special materials: everything that goes into a patient during surgery. For the high-tech end of that list, the country barely produces anything.

100%of the 51 active pacemaker registrations list only foreign manufacturers
0.1%of pacemaker imports covered by exports in 2025 (US$ 29.0M in, US$ 34.6k out)
US$ 2.80of stents exported for every US$ 100 imported in 2025
99%of the 102 active catheter registrations list only foreign manufacturers

Supply is concentrated too. Three suppliers took 84% of the value of public purchases of dual-chamber pacemakers, and 76% of mechanical heart valve imports came from a single country, the United States. For a distributor this is a supply-risk map: the exchange rate, import lead times and a handful of manufacturers decide whether a hospital gets its device on time.

One catalog code, prices thirteen times apart

Public buyers describe each item with a CATMAT code. It is tempting to treat the code as a product and compare prices across states. The data says not to. Under the same code for a titanium calcaneus locking plate, the cheapest 10% of purchases paid up to R$ 292 per unit and the most expensive 10% paid at least R$ 3,910.

In 11 of the 25 items I track, the 90th percentile is more than five times the 10th. A code like this bundles different brands, materials and specifications, so comparing two states mostly compares which brands they bought. That is why the pipeline runs a homogeneity check before any state comparison, and why I report one more result as a finding in its own right: no price gap between states passed every robustness rule. Seven were moderate signals, twelve were weak and six items had too few purchases.

A price difference is not evidence of wrongdoing. Volume, delivery terms, consignment, urgency and specification explain much of the spread. The project documents this limitation up front.

The reimbursement table is falling behind

SUS, Brazil's public health system, reimburses providers using a reference table (SIGTAP). The table for a thoracic aortic endoprosthesis has paid R$ 14,000 since at least January 2015, with no adjustment. In today's money that 2015 value is worth R$ 26,250, so the table has lost 47% in real terms.

The gap shows up in what hospitals actually pay. For the patellar component of a knee prosthesis, the median public purchase price was R$ 681.81 against a table value of R$ 345.46: 2.0× the table (95% CI 1.5× to 2.2×, 48 purchases by 34 agencies). The difference has to be absorbed somewhere else in the system.

How I built it

  • Pipeline: raw → bronze → silver → gold layers with Polars and DuckDB, schema checks with pandera at every layer, a retrying HTTP client with daily cache, and a monthly refresh on GitHub Actions.
  • Rules before findings: prices are compared only for the same code and unit, in medians, in constant BRL, with a minimum sample per state and cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals. Only findings that pass every rule are called strong.
  • Audit: an independent script recomputed the published numbers straight from the raw files, and individual purchases were checked by hand against the source.
  • Privacy: personal tax IDs (CPF) that show up in supplier fields are masked in the cleaned layers, and none is kept in the repository.
